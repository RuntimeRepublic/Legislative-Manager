# Kansans Launch Open, Public Effort to Draft the "One-Hour Rule": No Bill Voted On Until Its Text Has Been Public for an Hour

**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**
**November 1, 2026**

**KANSAS** — Runtime Republic today opened public work on the **One-Hour Rule**, a nonpartisan proposal that would bar the Kansas Legislature from voting on, amending or signing any bill until its exact text has been public for at least one hour.

Under current practice, a bill's text can be stripped out and replaced with new language on a different subject, the "gut and go," and final conference committee reports must be available to members only 30 minutes before a vote. A two-thirds vote of the members present can waive even that.

The One-Hour Rule would give every version of every bill a permanent public record, a time stamp and a digital "fingerprint," so any Kansan could confirm that the text lawmakers voted on is the text that was posted. In an emergency the Legislature could still move faster, but the text would have to be posted before the vote.

"Kansans fund every law. They should be able to read it before it's decided, not after," said James A. Hodges, who leads the effort. "We're writing this rule the way we think every bill should be written: in public, with every change signed and dated."

Today's work order opens the first research phase. Volunteers will map the constitutional provisions, joint rules and statutes involved, define key terms and verify every citation. The goal is a complete draft of all three layers (a joint rule, a statute and a constitutional amendment) by **November 30**, ready for legal review before the 2027 session.

**Kansans are invited to help.** No legal or technical background is needed. Volunteers read a free five-lesson course (about three minutes per lesson) and complete a five-minute readiness check.
